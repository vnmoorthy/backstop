# Aetna — CO-50 Not Medically Necessary

Aetna's CO-50 denial asserts the service was not medically necessary and is
therefore non-covered. These are won on the clinical record, not on the phone:
the goal of the call is to open a peer-to-peer review so the rendering physician
can speak to Aetna's medical director against the payer's own medical policy
criteria.

## Winning rebuttal

> This service was medically necessary and meets Aetna's own clinical-policy
> criteria. The documented diagnosis and clinical findings — failed
> conservative therapy, objective findings on examination, and supporting
> imaging — satisfy each element of the applicable medical policy bulletin. The
> determination that the service was not medically necessary is not supported by
> the record. We request a peer-to-peer review with the rendering physician
> before any final adverse determination.

## Required evidence

- Office notes documenting failed conservative treatment and objective findings.
- The exact Aetna Clinical Policy Bulletin number the denial relied on.
- Imaging or lab results that satisfy each medical-policy criterion.

## IVR path

1. Dial the Aetna provider services line.
2. Press `2` for "claims and authorizations".
3. Press `4` for "appeals and clinical review".
4. Enter the provider NPI followed by `#`.
5. Enter the member ID followed by `#`.
6. Say "peer to peer" or "medical necessity appeal".
7. Request the peer-to-peer scheduling line and provide the physician's direct
   callback number and availability window.
